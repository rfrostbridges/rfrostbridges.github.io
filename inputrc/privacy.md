# Inputrc privacy policy

Last changed: 27 October 2026

Inputrc is a text editor for iPhone, made by Robert Frost-Bridges, an individual developer in the
United Kingdom. This page says what happens to your information when you use it.

## In short

Inputrc collects no data. It has no account, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics and no
tracking. Inputrc itself never sends your text, your file names or anything about you to the
developer or to anyone else.

## Your files

Your files stay where you keep them. Files in Inputrc's own folder are on your iPhone (in the
Files app, under On My iPhone, Inputrc). A file you open from elsewhere, such as iCloud Drive or
another app's folder, is edited where it lives and is not copied. If that place is synced, for
example by iCloud, the syncing is done by that service, not by Inputrc.

Unsaved text is kept on your iPhone as a draft, so that it is not lost if Inputrc is closed. Your
settings are also kept on your iPhone. Deleting Inputrc deletes these, and the files in Inputrc's
own folder.

## The clipboard

Inputrc's kill ring is joined to the iPhone clipboard, as a computer's editor joins them: text you
cut or copy in Inputrc is also put on the clipboard, and when you paste (C-y) Inputrc reads the
clipboard, so that text copied in another app can be pasted. It reads the clipboard only when you
paste. Inputrc's Settings, Kill ring and clipboard, Apart, keeps the two separate, and then Inputrc's commands
leave the clipboard alone. (The iPhone's own Copy and Paste, from its menu, work as in any app.)

## Purchases

The free trial and the unlock are in-app purchases made through Apple's App Store. Apple handles
the payment and your Apple Account. Inputrc is told only whether the trial or the unlock belongs
to your Apple Account, and the developer never sees your payment details. Apple's own privacy
policy covers what Apple does: <https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/>.

## Information Apple may share

If you have chosen, in your iPhone's settings, to share analytics with app developers, Apple may
give the developer crash reports and anonymous usage figures for Inputrc. This is Apple's choice
to offer and yours to make; Inputrc adds nothing to it. You can change it in the iPhone's Settings
app (not Inputrc's): Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements.

People testing Inputrc through Apple's TestFlight may send feedback through TestFlight. Apple then
passes the feedback, with the tester's email address, to the developer.

## If you write to us

If you email robertfb-apps@posteo.uk (the address for all of the developer's apps), your address and message are used only to answer you and to
fix what you report. They are not shared or used for anything else, and are deleted when they are
no longer needed for that. The mail is held by Posteo, an email provider in Germany.

The **Get help or report a problem** button in Inputrc's Help only opens a new email in your own
mail app. It fills in Inputrc's version and the iOS version, and nothing else: no text or names
from your files. You see the whole email, can change or delete any of it, and nothing is sent
unless you send it.

## Children

Inputrc collects nothing from anyone, children included.

## Your rights

You can ask what information the developer holds about you (normally none, or only your emails),
and ask for it to be corrected or deleted, by writing to robertfb-apps@posteo.uk. In the United
Kingdom you may also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office: <https://ico.org.uk/>.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be on this page with a new date. Inputrc will not
start collecting data without saying so here first.
